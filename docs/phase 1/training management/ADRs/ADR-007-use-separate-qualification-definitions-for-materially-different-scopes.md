# Use Separate Qualification Definitions for Materially Different Scopes

## Context and Problem Statement

Related employee populations may require materially different qualification steps. Equipment-specific qualifications and different fall-protection scopes may share foundational training while authorizing different work.

## Decision Drivers

* Distinguish equipment-specific practical completion.
* Reuse common lockout/tagout training.
* Keep equipment-specific evidence identifiable.
* Avoid unnecessary conditional-rule complexity.

## Considered Options

* Create distinct qualification definitions for each materially different scope, with shared foundational requirements.
* Use one competency containing alternative combinations of qualifying criteria.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: **Create distinct qualification definitions for each materially different scope, with shared foundational requirements**, because the resulting record should state unambiguously what the employee is authorized to do. Related qualifications may be grouped under a common category for navigation and reporting.

## Consequences

* Equipment-specific practical completion can be represented explicitly.
* Common foundational training can be reused.
* The catalog will contain additional practical definitions.
* Qualification definitions will be more numerous but simpler to interpret.
