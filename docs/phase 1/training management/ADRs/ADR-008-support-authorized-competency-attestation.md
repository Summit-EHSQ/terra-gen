# Support Authorized Competency Attestation

## Context and Problem Statement

Some competent-person designations depend on a site manager's judgement of experience and skills against program criteria. These requirements are not necessarily evidenced by a conventional course or existing assessment form.

## Decision Drivers

* Accommodate judgement-based designation.
* Recognize work history and practical experience.
* Retain the criteria underlying a competency decision.
* Distinguish certification or designation from training completion.

## Considered Options

* Allow an authorized person to attest that applicable competency criteria have been met.
* Require formal course or test completion for every competency.
* Record only the designation without representing its criteria.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: **Allow an authorized person to attest that applicable competency criteria have been met**, because some designations rely on manager judgement of work history and skills.

Attestation may evidence experiential criteria or support a designation. It does not automatically remove other training requirements. Whether attestation grants the competency directly or satisfies one component within its requirement bundle remains unresolved.

## Consequences

* The model can reflect program requirements that rely on professional judgement.
* Authorized-person responsibilities need to be defined.
* Criteria and evidence capture require further design.
* A universal course-only model would not adequately represent these cases.

## More Information

Transcript reference: 42:15–44:18.

No specific attestation form, approval workflow, or permission group was selected. The absence of a current form was not a decision to prohibit a future form. See ADR-004 and ADR-006.
