# Support Both Direct Actions and Formal Findings

## Context and Problem Statement

A negative response may identify a straightforward deficiency requiring an action. Other issues, especially environmental compliance issues, may require a complete record of severity, classifications, immediate response, and possible self-reporting obligations. Always requiring a finding is excessive for routine issues, while linking every response directly to an action may omit important information.

## Decision Drivers

* Routine deficiencies should be quick to document and correct.
* Significant issues may require severity and classification.
* Environmental issues may trigger self-reporting.
* A finding may require multiple actions.
* Findings must trace to their originating response.

## Considered Options

* Create actions directly from every deficient answer.
* Require a finding for every deficient answer.
* Support either a direct action or a formal finding with related actions.
* Record findings only as comments.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: **Support either a direct action or a formal finding with one or more related actions**, because programs and issue types have different documentation requirements. Formal findings will retain traceability to the inspection and originating question.

## Consequences

* Routine issues avoid unnecessary data entry.
* Significant issues receive sufficient detail for reporting and follow-up.
* Corrective actions remain distinct from the condition that caused them.
* Reporting must account for both paths.
* Configuration or guidance must determine when a finding is required.

## More Information

Detailed design must determine whether the path is fixed by program, driven by severity, selected by the inspector, or determined by a combination of factors.
